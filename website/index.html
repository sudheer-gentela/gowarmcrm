Why both exist

The same gap, on both sides of the business.

Systems of record were built to answer one question: what happened? A CRM stores calls and stages. A project tool stores tasks and dates. Both do that job well, and that is not the problem.

The problem is the question nobody built a system to answer: what should happen next? On the revenue side, that gap is a deal that goes quiet for eighteen days while the CRM still reads "In Progress." On the delivery side, it is a project that stopped three weeks ago and a team whose work is invisible until someone asks. What is the execution gap? →

Neither is a people problem. Reps are not lazy and teams are not undisciplined. It is structural: the act of recording work has been separated from the act of doing it, so the record decays exactly as fast as the work speeds up — which is why it is always worst in the quarter you most need it.

Both products correct it the same way. Put the writing where the work already is. Produce a next action rather than a dashboard. Make a missing day obvious instead of silent, and stop letting anyone close something by declaring it closed. The longer argument is here →
