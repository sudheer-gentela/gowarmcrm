# GoWarm Work vs a Shared Spreadsheet

> Nearly every company under 500 people runs projects and daily work out of a shared sheet, and for a long time that is the correct decision. This page covers the point at which it stops being correct, the six things a spreadsheet structurally cannot do, what replaces each one, and when the sheet is still the right answer.

## What the spreadsheet is genuinely good at

It is free. Everyone already knows how to use it, so there is no adoption problem. It takes minutes to set up and can be reshaped mid-project without asking anyone. It imposes no opinion about how you work. For one project, one owner and a horizon under a month, a sheet is the right tool.

## Six things a sheet cannot do

1. **Tell absence from silence.** A blank cell might be a holiday, a quiet day, or someone who stopped filling it in six weeks ago. Without a working calendar underneath, no number derived from the sheet can be trusted. *Replaced by:* a schedule and holiday calendar per person, so compliance is days logged divided by working days.
2. **Enforce a sequence.** Nothing stops row 14 being marked done before row 9. *Replaced by:* task dependencies validated for circularity at creation, and stage gates that hold the next stage shut.
3. **Require evidence.** "Done" means whatever the person typing believes. *Replaced by:* a task closes with a file, link or note attached that cannot be quietly rewritten.
4. **Aggregate free text.** "Client call", "Call with client" and "customer discussion" become three lines in every summary. *Replaced by:* a controlled activity vocabulary with an "Other" escape hatch feeding a manager merge queue.
5. **Keep a ledger.** Any figure can be edited in place, so a cost record is a current opinion rather than a history. *Replaced by:* an append-only spend ledger of increments, rates snapshotted per entry, corrections booked as reversals, variations kept separate from the bill.
6. **Remember what a date used to be.** Change a date and the previous one is gone. *Replaced by:* a baseline frozen when the plan is committed, so drift is a measured difference.

## What has to survive the move

Replacements fail when they are heavier than the sheet. The floor stays at one line a day, written where the person already is. Nothing generates work, so there is no backlog. The activity list is meant to be edited in the first month. Setup is departments, schedules, a holiday calendar and a first activity list — most teams run within a week. A project checklist can be imported, and the baseline freezes when the plan is committed, so an in-flight project can move across.

## When to keep the spreadsheet

One project, one owner, a horizon under a month, and no decision riding on a number the sheet produces. Four conditions flip it: more than one person writes to the same record in the same week; the record has to outlive somebody's memory; a number from it is used to make a decision; or you cannot tell from the sheet whether work is happening at all.
