# Daily Work Tracking Without Timesheets

> Daily work tracking is a short written record of what a person did on a piece of work, made by that person on the day. It is not a timesheet and not an activity monitor. In GoWarm Work it is one line, written where the person already is, stored once and read on their own day, against the project task, and in their manager's rollup.

## The spreadsheet you already have

Almost every company under 500 people has a shared sheet for this. It fails for three separable reasons:

- **It is a separate job.** Describing your week in a sheet is work about work, so it competes with the work and loses. The version that survives is one where writing the line is part of doing the task.
- **Nothing is comparable.** "Client call", "Call with client" and "customer discussion" become three lines in any summary, so the sheet can be read but never counted.
- **Absence is indistinguishable from silence.** A blank cell might be a quiet day, a holiday, or someone who stopped filling it in six weeks ago. Without a working calendar underneath, the sheet supports no reliable statement.

## Say it once

An update posted on a project task *is* the person's daily work entry — the same stored row, not a copy. It is read by their own day view, by the task, and by the manager rollup. Editing it from any of the three edits the one row. Two stored copies would need synchronising, and the first time two screens disagree, both stop being believed.

## Nothing is generated

No scheduler creates a daily row. A day with no entry is an absence, not an unfinished item. Compliance is days logged divided by working days in the window, with the denominator taken from the person's schedule and the organisation's holiday calendar.

In one live system, a generated action queue for a single user grew from 389 to 541 to 583 open items and then sat unchanged for nineteen days, with every digest unread. That is the resting state of any tool that manufactures work.

## Controlled vocabulary with an escape hatch

People pick activity types from a list defined for the organisation, so rollups are countable. "Other" is always available so nobody is blocked from logging by an incomplete list. Everything entered as Other lands in a queue the manager promotes into the real list or merges into an existing entry, so the vocabulary grows out of what people actually do.

## What the manager sees

Each person's logging record beside their open and overdue project tasks, plus a full per-person timeline. Entries appear as they are written; there is no batch and no report to run.

## What it deliberately does not do

No timers, screenshots, idle detection, keystroke logging or productivity scores — not as a disabled setting, the capability does not exist. The measure is whether work was recorded on a working day and what it was. A record people are afraid of is a record nobody writes honestly.
