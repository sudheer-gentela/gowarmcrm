# GoWarm for Founders

> There is a size at which a founder stops seeing the work directly and starts receiving descriptions of it. Everything offered as a substitute — status decks, weekly reviews, dashboards, project tools — reports on the reporting rather than the work. GoWarm closes that gap on both sides of the business: GoWarm CRM for the pipeline, GoWarm Work for the delivery and the work that appears in no pipeline at all.

## The crossing

At twelve people you know what everyone is doing because you are in the room. At twenty-five you can reconstruct it in a morning of walking around. Past forty that stops — not because the team got worse or the founder got lazier. Direct observation has a ceiling.

What replaces it is description: a slide assembled last night, a sentence in a review, a dashboard whose definition nobody checked, a date last edited in March. The usual diagnoses — managers hiding things, teams lacking ownership, the founder needing to be more hands-on — are wrong and expensive. The cause is structural: the act of recording work has been separated from the act of doing it, so the record decays exactly as fast as the work speeds up.

## The same gap, both sides

- **Revenue side.** The CRM records what happened. The gap is a deal that goes quiet for eighteen days while the record still reads "In Progress".
- **Delivery side.** The project tool records the plan. The gap is a project that stopped three weeks ago and a team whose work is invisible until someone asks.

Both are records of the past. Neither tells a person what to do on Tuesday morning.

## Two products, one platform

**GoWarm CRM** sits above Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho or Dynamics, runs nightly diagnostics across deals, contracts, cases, handovers and prospects, and gives every rep a ranked list of what to do first. Signals are read from email, calendar and meeting patterns rather than only what was logged.

**GoWarm Work** carries projects with stages, gates and a baseline frozen at the start, and daily work written once by the person doing it and read on their own day, against the task, and in the manager rollup. Tasks close with evidence attached. Nothing generates work, so no queue grows past the point of being read.

Modules are enabled per organisation. Either side runs without the other.

## What changes for the founder

- You stop being the integration layer between functions that each see half the picture.
- You stop asking for status, and the weekly review becomes a decision meeting.
- You find out about a stalled deal or a stopped project on the day, not at the QBR.
- People whose work is currently invisible become assessable, in both directions, without being watched.

## What this deliberately is not

No timers, screenshots, idle detection or productivity scores — the capability does not exist. Those products answer whether people are working rather than what the company is doing, and that question corrodes what it measures. A record people are afraid of is a record nobody writes honestly.

## Five checks that cost nothing

1. When did a project's dates last change — not when it was last discussed?
2. What did someone in a non-revenue function do last Tuesday?
3. In whichever tool sends the most reminders: how many open items, how old is the oldest, and were the last digests opened?
4. Find something marked complete last quarter and ask what proves it.
5. Where were the last three decisions actually made?

The test underneath all five: if everyone stopped trying to keep you informed tomorrow, would you still know what happened this week?
