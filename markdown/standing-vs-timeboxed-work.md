# Standing vs Timeboxed Work

> Work asks two independent questions: whose is it, and does it end. Customer or internal is one axis; timeboxed or standing is the other. Almost every project tool models the first and assumes the answer to the second, which is why retainers sit at ninety per cent forever and delivery numbers cannot be trusted.

## The four combinations

All four occur and all four are ordinary:

- **Customer, timeboxed** — a client implementation with a go-live. Baseline frozen, gates between stages, drift measured against the plan.
- **Internal, timeboxed** — a migration with an owner and a date. Counts as delivery, not overhead.
- **Customer, standing** — a retainer or managed service. Never completes; retired when the arrangement ends.
- **Internal, standing** — training, compliance, upkeep, support. Real work, kept out of delivery statistics.

When a tool offers only the timeboxed shape, standing work does not disappear. It gets forced into a project that is opened once and never closed, and from that point it corrupts everything the tool reports.

## What one never-closing item does

A worked example. Three finite projects at 100, 80 and 60 per cent, plus a support retainer permanently at 90 per cent and permanently overdue.

- **Counted together:** average complete 82.5 per cent, one of four overdue. Neither figure describes anything real.
- **Counted separately:** delivery is three items averaging 80 per cent with nothing overdue; the retainer is reported on its own terms — attended on 18 of 20 working days.

Nobody entered bad data. The retainer is at ninety per cent because someone had to fill a field that should not have existed, and overdue because a date was required for something with no end. The distortion is created by the model.

It also compounds. Once the overdue list contains items that can never not be overdue, people stop reading it — which is how genuinely late projects become invisible.

## The three workarounds, and why they fail

1. **Roll the date forward monthly.** Works, and teaches everyone that dates in the system are decorative — a lesson that then applies to real deadlines.
2. **Keep it out of the tool.** The dashboard gets clean and a meaningful share of company effort becomes invisible, including to whoever is planning capacity.
3. **Create a fake monthly project.** The numbers work; continuity is lost, so nobody can see what happened on that account across a year.

## How to tell which you are looking at

Not by duration. Ask: is there a definition of done, not just a date? Would finishing it be good news or simply an ending? Does effort ramp toward a close, or repeat? What happens if nobody touches it for a month — does it slip, or degrade quietly?

Two common mistakes: treating a retainer as timeboxed because the contract has an end date, and treating an internal project as standing because it has no customer.

## How GoWarm Work handles it

Tracking mode is a property of the project, set independently of whether it belongs to a customer.

- **Timeboxed:** frozen baseline, stage gates, drift measured against the original plan, counted in delivery statistics.
- **Standing:** no completion percentage, retired rather than completed, excluded from delivery statistics, judged on whether it was worked on during the working days it should have been.

Standing work is not second-class — it is fully tracked and logged against, and appears in the manager rollup and each person's timeline. It simply does not appear in figures that assume a finish line.
